## Findings that change the design

1. **The default config's first rounds are dead.** Round *n* runs at sim hour `n × minutes_per_round / 60`,
   starting at 00:00. The generated config makes 00:00–05:00 off-peak, and no agent has those hours in its
   `active_hours`. So rounds 0–3 had **0 eligible agents** (`run_parallel_simulation.py:1071` skips them). A default
   3-round turn would produce only the seed posts. For S1 I shifted the clock by 12 h in `simulation_config.json`
   (every `active_hours`, `peak_hours`, `off_peak_hours`, `morning_hours` and `work_hours` list, mod 24), so that
   rounds 0–2 behave like 12:00–14:00. The config before and after is in `s1/simulation_config.{orig,shifted}.json`.
   The pipeline has to do the same, or set `active_hours` itself.
2. **Every run starts from an empty world.** The run script deletes `twitter_simulation.db` before each start
   (`run_parallel_simulation.py:1153`, `run_twitter_simulation.py:586`), whether or not `force` is set. `force`
   also deletes `actions.jsonl` and `run_state.json` (`simulation_runner.py:1365`). Nothing from turn *n* survives
   into turn *n+1* except what we write into `initial_posts`. Agents also keep no memory, since profiles are static.
3. **Restarting a Twitter-only sim through the API is blocked.** `/start` on a sim that isn't `ready` calls
   `_check_simulation_prepared`, which requires `reddit_profiles.json` (`app/api/simulation.py:296–301`). A
   Twitter-only prepare never writes that file. The response was *"Simulation not ready. Current status: completed.
   Please call /prepare first."* I worked around it by setting `"status": "ready"` in the sim's `state.json`.
   That skips the `force` cleanup, so:
   - **`/run-status` goes stale.** The old `actions.jsonl` is re-read, so the new run showed `twitter_completed: true`,
     round 3/3 at +0 s and 111 actions (S1 and S2 mixed). Watch `actions.jsonl` for the new `simulation_end`
     instead, or delete the old `twitter/actions.jsonl` before starting, which is exactly what `force` does.
   - Cleaner fixes are for the Lead to choose: a one-line source fix (check files per enabled platform), or Twitter
     and Reddit sims, which would double the cost per round.
4. **Only `initial_posts` injects at run time.** `hot_topics`, `narrative_direction` and `scheduled_events` are
   read only by the config generator; nothing in `backend/scripts/` reads them. On Twitter, the seed step is a dict
   keyed by agent (`run_parallel_simulation.py:1187`), so each agent posts **one seed post at most**. 16 seeds were
   generated and 15 went out. There is no live-injection command: IPC supports only `interview`, `batch_interview`
   and `close_env`.
5. **Injected events need sourcing.** Two agents (OpenAI, Canadian media) treated the US government post as
   unconfirmed: "no agency statement, order number or effective date". Game-event posts should carry those details,
   or come from two or more official accounts.
6. **The interview number is anchored to the persona, not to the turn.** Alarm stayed flat (5.83 → 5.66) after a
   major event. This was not a controlled A/B: the S2 world also had different seeds. The 0–10 answer alone is
   probably too inert for an alarm meter. The engagement share on the event post (19–23%) or a per-post classifier
   reacts more. An event-specific question might work but is untested.
7. **The 164 s first round in S1 was a one-off.** S2's first round took 9.8 s. The cause isn't confirmed.
   The local `Twitter/twhin-bert-base` recommender loads in both runs, so it was either a first-load cache cost or
   one slow LLM call. Start the demo with a warm-up run.
8. **Minor issues:** `actions.jsonl` logs the seed posts again under round 1, so subtract them. Some API messages
   are still Chinese despite `Accept-Language: en` (`prepare/status` "已完成", `close-env`). One stray Chinese
   word ("运营") appears in the Philadelphia police persona. The Twitter CSV has no country field, so the country
   check used `POST /api/simulation/generate-profiles` in Reddit format (6 MediaOutlet profiles, $0.0078). The rogue
   model's hidden reasoning drives its 3–10 s latency; `reasoning` settings could cut it but are untested.

## What it means for "one game turn = one MiroFish run"

**It's feasible on time and cost, but each run is a fresh world, so continuity is our job.**

- **Per turn:** `close-env` (2.5 s), then the config edit, then `start` (about 2 s to spawn), then 3 rounds
  (about 30 s), then `interview/all` (10–15 s). That's **about 45–50 s and about $0.03** (run $0.018 plus
  interview $0.013–0.017). The Rogue decision (about 5 s, $0.0005) can run during the sim. A 4-turn demo costs
  about $0.12 plus one prepare.
- **Per game:** one prepare (2.3 min, $0.05 for 29 agents), plus the clock shift from finding 1.
- **The pipeline must:**
  1. Carry the world forward by writing each turn's `initial_posts` as the game event plus the previous turn's top
     posts, with at most one per agent.
  2. Track run completion from `actions.jsonl`, not `/run-status`, unless it uses `force` or a source fix.
  3. Restart a Twitter-only sim through the workaround in finding 3, or through a fix the Lead approves.
- **Spend is not the risk:** $0.134 covered all five tests. The risks are the stale status, the empty-world reset
  and how inert the interview alarm is.

## Method notes and deviations

- I ran **S3 before S2**. `interview/all` needs the environment alive, and S2's restart wipes S1's world. S3b
  (after S2) and the country check were extra tests, each under $0.02.
- I kept GovernmentAgency in S1, because 29 agents (12 of them GovernmentAgency) cost only $0.049 to prepare.
- `platform: "parallel"` was used on the Twitter-only sim. `"twitter"` runs `run_twitter_simulation.py`, which
  writes no `actions.jsonl`, so `/run-status`, `/actions` and `/timeline` would stay empty. With `"parallel"` the
  Reddit half exits at once because it has no profiles.
- **S2 injection.** Initial posts: US government (agent 16) "…emergency caps on AI training compute… above 10^26
  FLOP now requires a federal licence…" and CNN (agent 12) "BREAKING: The US imposes emergency caps…". Four
  carry-over posts from S1's most-engaged posts went in alongside them, and "US emergency caps on AI training
  compute" was prepended to `hot_topics`.
- **What I edited** in MiroFish data, only for `sim_908b44137fec`: `simulation_config.json` (the clock shift and
  `event_config`) and `state.json` (`status`). I didn't edit any MiroFish source and didn't restart the backend. The
  spike environment is closed (`runner_status: completed`).
